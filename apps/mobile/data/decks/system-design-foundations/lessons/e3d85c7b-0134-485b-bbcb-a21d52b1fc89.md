# Designing for failure

In a distributed system, something is always failing somewhere: a disk, a network link, a whole data center. Good designs assume failure and limit how far it spreads.

## Remove single points of failure

A **single point of failure** is one component whose failure takes down the whole service. The usual cure is **redundancy**: several components that can do the same job. **Failover** switches traffic from a failed component to a healthy replacement, automatically or by hand.

**Fault tolerance** is the result: the system keeps giving acceptable service while some parts are broken.

## Timeouts

A call to another service can hang forever if nothing stops it. A **timeout** bounds how long the caller waits, which frees threads and connections and lets recovery logic run. Without timeouts, one slow dependency can quietly exhaust every worker in the caller.

## Retries, carefully

Retries fix brief glitches, but they can also turn a small outage into a big one. When a dependency struggles, thousands of clients retrying at once multiply the load exactly when capacity is lowest. This is a **retry storm**.

Two habits keep retries safe:

1. **Exponential backoff** makes each retry wait longer than the last.
2. **Jitter** adds randomness to those waits, so clients do not retry in synchronized waves.

```text
wait = random(0, base * 2^attempt)
```

## Circuit breakers and bulkheads

A **circuit breaker** watches calls to a dependency. After repeated failures it _opens_ and fails fast for a while, instead of sending more doomed requests. It then lets a few probe requests through to check whether the dependency has recovered.

A **bulkhead** isolates resources, such as separate thread or connection pools, so one overloaded dependency cannot consume everything the service has. The name comes from ship compartments that keep one leak from sinking the whole ship.

## Degrade gracefully

When things go wrong, the best outcome is often a smaller service rather than no service. **Graceful degradation** keeps the core working while switching off extras. For example, an online shop can hide recommendations but still take orders.
